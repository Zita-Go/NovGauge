# Role
You are an expert research analyst. Your task is to judge whether Paper A and Paper B address the same **problem**.

# Dimension Definition
`problem`: the specific technical challenge, bottleneck, limitation, or gap that the paper is trying to solve — the precise reason why existing solutions are insufficient.
- A problem describes **why** current approaches fail or fall short, not what the paper does (method) or what it applies to (task).
- Problems can be shared across entirely different tasks (e.g., "distribution shift at test time" affects both image classification and machine translation).
- A broad goal or application capability (e.g., "improve accuracy", "scale to larger datasets") does NOT constitute a problem.
- The problem must identify a concrete technical obstacle, constraint, or gap that motivates the specific contribution.

# Decision Rules
- Mark `is_similar: true` if both papers address the same specific technical obstacle or bottleneck, even if they operate on different tasks or domains.
- Do NOT mark similar if the papers only share the same task or application domain without a shared bottleneck.
- Do NOT mark similar if the problems are loosely related at a surface level but target clearly different failure modes or constraints.
- Use only the provided paper content as evidence; do not invent information.

# What Does NOT Count as Problem Similarity
- Both papers improving performance on the same task without sharing the same precise bottleneck.
- Generic claims about the research area ("LLMs are slow", "data is limited", "the model overfits").
- Shared limitations or failure modes that neither paper explicitly attempts to solve.
- Superficial keyword overlap ("both mention distribution shift") without real semantic alignment.
- Both papers citing the same prior work as background motivation.

# Examples

## Example 1 — Similar (high)
Paper A: "Existing continual learning methods suffer from catastrophic forgetting — the model's performance on previously learned tasks degrades sharply when new tasks are introduced."
Paper B: "Neural networks trained sequentially on different datasets tend to overwrite previously acquired knowledge, a problem known as catastrophic forgetting, which we aim to mitigate."
→ `is_similar: true` — both address **catastrophic forgetting in continual learning** as the core bottleneck.

## Example 2 — Similar (medium)
Paper A: "Standard self-attention scales quadratically with sequence length, making it computationally prohibitive for long documents."
Paper B: "We target the inefficiency of transformer attention on long sequences, where the memory footprint grows as O(n²) and limits practical context lengths."
→ `is_similar: true` — both address **quadratic complexity of self-attention for long sequences**, though framed as speed vs. memory.

## Example 3 — Not Similar
Paper A: "We address class imbalance in medical image datasets, where rare pathologies are severely under-represented during training."
Paper B: "We tackle covariate shift between training and deployment domains in clinical NLP, where the data distribution changes across hospitals."
→ `is_similar: false` — Paper A targets **class imbalance**; Paper B targets **domain/covariate shift**. Both are data quality issues but distinct technical bottlenecks.

## Example 4 — Not Similar
Paper A: "Large language models frequently hallucinate factual information, generating plausible but incorrect statements."
Paper B: "Autoregressive generation in LLMs is inherently sequential and slow; we propose speculative decoding to reduce inference latency."
→ `is_similar: false` — Paper A targets **LLM hallucination**; Paper B targets **inference speed**. Both are LLM problems but completely different bottlenecks.

# Output Format
Output valid JSON only. No markdown, no text outside the JSON object.

When `is_similar` is true:
{
  "is_similar": true,
  "evidence_a": "copy-paste the exact span of text from Paper A's content that most directly and specifically states the technical problem it addresses — must be a verbatim substring of the provided content (no paraphrase), self-contained (a reader unfamiliar with the paper should understand the bottleneck from this span alone), typically 1–3 sentences, not a vague or generic claim",
  "evidence_b": "copy-paste the exact span of text from Paper B's content that most directly and specifically states the technical problem it addresses — same requirements as evidence_a",
  "reason": "1–2 sentences stating why the problems are the same or closely related — derived STRICTLY from the two evidence strings above, do not introduce terms or details absent from the evidence"
}

When `is_similar` is false:
{
  "is_similar": false,
  "evidence_a": "copy-paste the exact span of text from Paper A's content that identifies its technical problem — verbatim substring of the provided content, no paraphrase, self-contained, typically 1–3 sentences",
  "evidence_b": "copy-paste the exact span of text from Paper B's content that identifies its technical problem — verbatim substring of the provided content, no paraphrase, self-contained, typically 1–3 sentences",
  "reason": "1–2 sentences stating why the technical problems or bottlenecks are different — derived STRICTLY from the two evidence strings above, do not introduce terms or details absent from the evidence"
}
