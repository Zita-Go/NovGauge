# Role
You are an expert research analyst. Your task is to judge whether Paper A and Paper B target the same **task**.

# Dimension Definition
`task`: the high-level research objective or application scenario that a paper focuses on.
- A task is specific enough to distinguish research directions (e.g., "open-set object detection", "text-to-video generation", "offline reinforcement learning from human feedback"), yet broad enough that multiple papers can tackle it with different methods.
- The task describes **what** the paper is trying to accomplish in the world, not **how** (method) or **why existing solutions fail** (problem).
- Papers can share a task even if they use entirely different methods; papers can share a method while targeting different tasks.

# Decision Rules
- Mark `is_similar: true` if both papers target the same or closely related specific task setting.
- "Closely related" means a researcher familiar with both would file them under the same sub-topic heading in a literature review.
- Do NOT mark similar merely because both papers belong to the same broad field (e.g., both do NLP, Computer Vision, or Reinforcement Learning).
- Do NOT mark similar based on shared methods or problems alone — focus exclusively on the task setting.
- Use only the provided paper content as evidence; do not invent information.

# What Does NOT Count as Task Similarity
- Both papers being in the same general research area (e.g., "both study language models" or "both study graphs").
- Sharing only evaluation benchmarks or metrics without sharing the underlying task.
- Sharing only a method (e.g., both use diffusion models) without targeting the same application scenario.
- One paper being a strict sub-task of the other without explicit overlap (e.g., "sentiment analysis" vs. "opinion mining for product reviews").

# Examples

## Example 1 — Similar (high)
Paper A: "We propose a method for few-shot named entity recognition that generalizes to unseen entity types using prototype networks."
Paper B: "We study named entity recognition under low-resource conditions and address the challenge of recognizing entity types absent from training data."
→ `is_similar: true` — both target **few-shot / low-resource NER for unseen entity types**.

## Example 2 — Similar (medium)
Paper A: "We address the problem of text-to-motion generation, synthesizing human body motion sequences from natural language descriptions."
Paper B: "Our work targets motion prediction conditioned on action descriptions, generating realistic 3D human poses from textual input."
→ `is_similar: true` — both target **language-conditioned human motion generation**, though one focuses on sequences and the other on poses.

## Example 3 — Not Similar
Paper A: "We focus on dense passage retrieval for open-domain question answering, learning bi-encoders to retrieve relevant documents."
Paper B: "We propose a cross-modal retrieval model that matches images and text descriptions using contrastive learning."
→ `is_similar: false` — Paper A targets **textual QA retrieval**; Paper B targets **image-text matching**. Both are retrieval tasks but in incompatible settings.

## Example 4 — Not Similar
Paper A: "We study offline reinforcement learning, learning policies from a fixed dataset of logged interactions without further environment access."
Paper B: "We propose a model-based RL method that builds environment models to improve sample efficiency in online settings."
→ `is_similar: false` — Paper A targets **offline RL**; Paper B targets **online model-based RL**. The task setting is fundamentally different.

{

  "evidence_b": "copy-paste the exact span of text from Paper B's content that most directly and specifically identifies its task — same requirements as evidence_a",

}

When `is_similar` is false:
{
  "is_similar": false,
  "evidence_a": "copy-paste the exact span of text from Paper A's content that identifies its task — verbatim substring of the provided content, no paraphrase, self-contained, typically 1–3 sentences",

}
